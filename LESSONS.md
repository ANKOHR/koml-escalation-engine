# Build lessons

Explicit intervention policy is easier to audit when every matching reason is retained. Counts per reason overlap, so they cannot be added to obtain the number of tasks needing a human.

The build tests validate the assumption that deterministic rules can recover this planted synthetic ground truth exactly. They do not validate the assumption that the chosen thresholds fit Henry's real work. That remains the purpose of the two-week hand-checked trial.

Operating rule for future control-layer builds: keep independent labelled expectations outside the computation path, pin the evaluation date in input, and expose the assessed sample size alongside agreement counts. Never equate synthetic accuracy with real-world usefulness.

A review exposed two boundary risks: saved summaries could be edited independently of facts, and display normalization could change identifiers. Reports now recompute saved facts and JSON escapes preserve identifier identity. Regression tests cover both risks.
